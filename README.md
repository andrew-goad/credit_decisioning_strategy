# Credit Decisioning Strategy Simulator

Would you approve a $12,839.56 auto loan for an applicant with a 654 credit score? Two lending policies evaluated that same synthetic application. With a modeled default probability of 7.9007%, the baseline sent it to manual review. The growth policy approved it and added $456.49 of modeled expected loss to the offer portfolio. The simulator lets you trace that decision and test what a change in strategy would do across 50,000 applications before introducing it in production.

**[Follow the auto applicant](https://andrew-goad.github.io/credit_decisioning_strategy/)** · [Compare the two policies across the portfolio](https://andrew-goad.github.io/credit_decisioning_strategy/?comparison=CG02#portfolio) · [Verify the decision and its inputs](https://andrew-goad.github.io/credit_decisioning_strategy/?trace=decisions.229#application-lesson)

## What changed for the portfolio?

In the baseline-versus-growth comparison (CG02), 2,839 applicants gained an offer and 1,476 lost one. The growth policy made **1,363 additional offers** on the same 50,000 requests. Offered exposure increased by **$238.42 million** and modeled expected loss increased by **$8.68 million**. For the opening applicant, the $322.14 monthly payment used 3.19% of monthly income. The review band around each policy’s risk cutoff explains the change in outcome.

Another experiment expanded entry into counteroffer testing and tightened offer sizing. With standard requests (CG18), the challenger made **589 more offers** while offering **$187.54 million less**. With larger requests (CG20), it made **5,651 more offers** and offered **$62.23 million more**. Matching applicants reveals how much movement came from new offers, lost offers and resized offers to applicants who received an offer under both policies. ## How the experiment works

| Stage | What you can learn |
| --- | --- |
| **1 · Generate applications** | Module 1 v2.0 generates products, borrower characteristics and requested terms. It estimates monthly payment, a synthetic default probability and product-specific loss severity. [Module 1 requirements](Module_1_Synthetic_Application_%26_Risk_Modeling_Engine/v2.0/docs/Enterprise_Credit_Decisioning_Simulator_Module1_Synthetic_Application_Risk_Engine_BRD_v2.0.pdf) |
| **2 · Apply lending policy** | Module 2 v1.0 tests the original request against the selected policy. It approves, tests a smaller offer, refers for review or declines. A counteroffer must clear the sizing and feasibility checks after rounding. [See why a $19,000 counteroffer cleared a limit by $9.84](https://andrew-goad.github.io/credit_decisioning_strategy/?comparison=CG18&app=APP-00040465#paired-case) · [Module 2 requirements](Module_2_Credit_Policy_Strategy_%26_Decision_Outcome_Simulation_Engine/v1.0/docs/MODULE_2_BUSINESS_REQUIREMENTS.md) |
| **3 · Compare like with like** | The original DBeaver campaign recorded 39 runs, each with 50,000 IDs, across 19 source scenarios. The 20 registered comparisons pair policies on the same source applications. The same ID can have different requested terms in another scenario. [Comparison registry](Module_2_Credit_Policy_Strategy_%26_Decision_Outcome_Simulation_Engine/v1.0/outputs/module2_credit_policy_strategy_decision_outcome_simulation_engine_v1.0_OUTPUT_strategy_comparison_campaign_registry.csv) · [Module 2 validation](Module_2_Credit_Policy_Strategy_%26_Decision_Outcome_Simulation_Engine/v1.0/tests/MODULE_2_VALIDATION_SUMMARY.md) |
| **4 · Design a comparison** | The historical campaign used two SQL programs in PostgreSQL. The project guide explains DBeaver execution, parameter settings, preflight checks and how to inspect each run’s outputs. [DBeaver guide and parameter reference](https://andrew-goad.github.io/credit_decisioning_strategy/#parameter-guides) · [Technical artifact map](PROJECT_ARTIFACT_MAP.md) |

The full explorer contains **1,950,000 simulated decisions**. You can follow any application ID from 1 to 50,000 across all 39 runs. Selected teaching cases add a detailed risk-factor or counteroffer calculation. A full approval or feasible counteroffer counts as an offer. Actual customer acceptance, booked balances, defaults, recoveries and profitability would require observed performance.
