The most important findings

1. The strongest strategy story is about consequences—not favorable-looking metrics

One particularly useful example is the manual-review-reduction strategy.

On the same 50,000-application synthetic baseline, manual-review routing declined from 7,535 to 5,316, a reduction of 2,219 cases, or 29.45% of the baseline review population. The professionally meaningful interpretation is:

It is not “I reduced review costs by 29%.” No realized cost saving, turnaround improvement, or completed human-review outcome is established.

2. Counteroffer feasibility is a major differentiator

The counteroffer design goes beyond reducing an amount until a case appears acceptable. It considers product limits, affordability targets, and modeled Expected-Loss constraints; enforces minimum viable exposure and allowed reductions; rounds the candidate; and checks feasibility again.
