The specific context gaps Leap 7 should target

There are several recurring patterns I would use as explicit review categories.

Metric without sufficient business meaning. The fix is not “add a disclaimer.” It is to establish the business/control problem first and ask whether the number still earns its place.

Method without decision consequence. OCC is especially susceptible to this. A hiring reader should know what analytical question economists or exam teams needed answered before reading about segmentation, event definitions, or model inputs.

Technology without user value. Census, Tableau, Power BI, SAS feeds, and AI tools become much stronger when we say what the analyst, executive, reviewer, or customer could understand or do because of them.

Execution without judgment. Many people can say they built something. Your stronger differentiator is that you challenged treatments, framed alternatives, resolved ambiguity, designed controls, and translated decisions into execution.

Outcome without mechanism. “Saved ~$60K per submission” is stronger when the reader sees that you challenged an overbroad strategy, designed targeted execution, preserved customer protection, and then produced the cost benefit.

Scale clutter. When three or four denominators appear in a paragraph, the reader starts doing accounting instead of following the story. Leap 7 should decide which one or two measures best prove the point and move the others to a more technical surface—or omit them.

Career stages that read as isolated jobs. Leap 7 should make the progression more intentional: Census taught productized analytical delivery; OCC added rigorous credit-risk analysis, challenge, and model/data validation; Wells added consequence, executive judgment, governance, and sustained implementation; the portfolio demonstrates current technical/AI-enabled capability. That arc should be visible without literally repeating it everywhere.
