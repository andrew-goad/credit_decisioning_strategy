Most of the hyphens are appropriate, but I would make a few changes to reduce over-hyphenation and make the letter read more naturally.

## I would keep these hyphens

These are appropriate because they function as compound modifiers before a noun:

```text
customer-level data
enterprise-scale analytics
high-stakes enterprise analytics
production-ready reporting
proof-of-insurance intake
executive-ready RISK / FACT framework
line-of-business Teradata sources
account-month reporting outcomes
PostgreSQL-based Enterprise Credit Decisioning Strategy Simulator
decision-outcome tradeoffs
next-generation analytics capabilities
credit-focused intelligence
loss-forecasting insight
```

## I would change or reconsider these

### 1. “senior-leadership decision-making”

Technically defensible, but it reads a little stiff. I would change it.

Current:

```text
senior-leadership decision-making
```

Better:

```text
senior leadership decisions
```

So the sentence becomes:

```text
...frameworks that support risk management, scenario interpretation, loss forecasting, model governance, and senior leadership decisions.
```

### 2. “credit-risk research” and “credit-risk modeling systems”

These are not wrong, but in financial services **credit risk** is often treated as a standard noun phrase. I would usually write:

```text
credit risk research
credit risk modeling systems
```

instead of:

```text
credit-risk research
credit-risk modeling systems
```

The hyphenated version is grammatically acceptable when modifying another noun, but the unhyphenated version reads more natural for industry language.

### 3. “loss-of-use time value of money”

This is the one I would definitely revise. The phrase is understandable, but the hyphen pattern is clunky.

Current:

```text
loss-of-use time value of money
```

Better:

```text
time value of money calculations for loss-of-use relief
```

So that sentence becomes:

```text
In the Collateral Protection Insurance remediation, I designed and owned analytical solutions supporting proof-of-insurance intake, Direct and Indirect Auto redress recalculation EUCTs, time value of money calculations for loss-of-use relief, and credit reporting remediation.
```

That is cleaner and avoids a pileup of compound terms.

### 4. “account-lineage logic”

This is technically fine, but I would probably remove the hyphen because **account lineage** is clear as a noun phrase.

Current:

```text
account-lineage logic
```

Better:

```text
account lineage logic
```

## Best revised versions of the affected sentences

I would adjust these three passages:

