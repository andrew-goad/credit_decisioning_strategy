Product mix and credit-score mix

Choose the fraction of applications in each category. Enter a decimal share from 0 to 1, for example 0.25 for 25%. The SQL checks each five-field total against 1 with an absolute tolerance of 0.000010. Keep every individual share within the probability range. Each mix uses its own repeatable draw, so realized counts can vary around the targets.

Annual rates and revolving payments

Each product starts with a positive annual rate entered as a decimal. The engine adds a score-band premium, pricing variation and the common shift. It then applies product-specific rate limits before calculating payments.

Rate shock and revolving payment assumptions
InputBaselineRange and effect
rate_shift_bpsINTEGER · 0Signed whole basis points. +100 adds one percentage point to each raw annual rate. Section 3 imposes no magnitude limit.
revolving_base_min_payment_pctNUMERIC(12,6) · 0.020 to 0.10 inclusive. Base monthly share of the requested revolving line.

Revolving monthly payment = requested line × [base payment share + (annual rate ÷ 12 × passthrough factor)]. For a $10,000 line at 24% APR, the baseline payment assumptions give $10,000 × (0.02 + 0.02 × 0.50) = $300 per month.

Loss given default by product

Loss given default (LGD) is the fraction of the balance the model assumes a lender loses if that borrower defaults. Enter a decimal strictly between 0 and 1. The same product-level assumption applies to every application of that product in the run.

Modeled loss on the request = requested amount × estimated default probability × LGD. Moving auto LGD from 0.45 to 0.55 raises loss on a $10,000 request at 8% estimated default probability from $360 to $440. Module 2 can then test whether an offer still meets its loss limit.
