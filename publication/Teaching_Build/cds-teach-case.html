Why did one auto loan move from review to approval?

In the baseline-versus-growth experiment, a synthetic applicant with a credit score of 654 requested $12,839.56 for an auto loan. Both policies saw the same application. Follow the numbers for APP-00032476.

Read the application

The estimated monthly payment was $322.14. Dividing annual income of $121,227.52 by 12 gives $10,102.29 in monthly income. The new payment would use 3.19% of that income. This is the payment-to-income ratio for this loan.

Follow the policy rule

How each policy treated the same estimated default probability of 7.9007%
PolicyApproval cutoff and borderline rangeRecorded decision
Baseline8% cutoff. The borderline range was 7.60% to 8.40%.Manual review. The applicant fell inside the range. The record gives “borderline profile” as the primary reason.
Growth10% cutoff. The borderline range moved to 9.50% to 10.50%.Approved as requested. The applicant fell below the range and cleared the other approval checks.

Each range extends 5% of the cutoff in either direction. For an 8% cutoff, that distance is 0.4 percentage points. This explains how an applicant just below the cutoff could still reach a reviewer.

Carry one decision into the portfolio

The model estimates a 7.9007% probability of default and a 45% loss of the amount lent if default occurs. Its expected loss on the requested loan is $12,839.56 × 0.079007 × 0.45 = $456.49, rounded to cents.

Under baseline, the decision was still open, so the engine left the amount offered and its modeled loss blank. Under growth, this application contributed one simulated offer, $12,839.56 in offered credit and $456.49 in modeled expected loss to the portfolio. Actual repayment would require later performance data.
