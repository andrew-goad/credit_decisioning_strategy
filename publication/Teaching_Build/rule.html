Where did the 7.9007% come from?

Module 1 drew the near-prime band, generated a score of 654 within it and used the band’s starting default probability of 6%. It also generated the borrower’s credit utilization (57.88%) and debt-to-income ratio (32.81%). Utilization multiplied the estimate by 1.25. The DTI multiplier was 1.00. Three recent inquiries and five credit accounts each contributed a factor of 1.05. The requested payment used $322.14 ÷ $10,102.29 = 3.19% of monthly income. That payment-to-income ratio contributed a factor of 0.95, and the requested amount relative to income contributed 1.03. The remaining profile multipliers were 1. A repeatable adjustment for this application was 0.976486.

6% × 1.25 × 1.05 × 1.05 × 0.95 × 1.03 × 0.976486 ≈ 7.9007%. This case stayed in the model’s normal range. These multipliers are assumptions in a synthetic model. Estimating real default probabilities would require observed repayment outcomes.

What would approval add to the portfolio?

For a baseline auto loan, the simulator assumes the lender loses $45 of every $100 outstanding if the borrower defaults. That 45% is the loss given default (LGD). Weight it by the chance of default to estimate the loss on the offer.

$12,839.56 × 7.9007% × 45% = $456.49
Amount exposed × probability of default × loss given default

Baseline leaves the file in review. Growth adds one offer, $12,839.56 of offered credit and $456.49 of modeled loss. Customer take-up and repayment would determine the realized result.

This borrower was one of 2,839 applicants who gained an offer under Growth. Another 1,476 lost one. Across the same 50,000 applicants, the net increase was 1,363 offers. Inspect the Growth comparison (CG02).

Why 45%, and when does it change?

Module 1 sets a loss rate for each product. The model assumes a lender can recover more from a secured mortgage than from an unsecured personal loan. These are adjustable assumptions. Calibrating them for a real portfolio would require observed defaults and recoveries.
