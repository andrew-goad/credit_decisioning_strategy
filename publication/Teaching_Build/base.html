Findings and insights

Offer counts rose in both scenarios. Total credit offered moved in opposite directions.

@@EXPOSURE_SUMMARY@@

Each policy evaluated the same 50,000 requests within its comparison. CG18 used the standard amounts. In CG20, 41,971 of those applicants requested more. The challenger widened entry to counteroffer testing and tightened offer sizing.

Which counteroffer rules changed?

More applicants with severe requested terms could qualify for a counteroffer test. For home equity lines, the maximum offer fell from $175,000 to $150,000, the target payment-to-income ratio from 22% to 20%, and the maximum modeled loss on an offer from $7,000 to $5,600. These comparisons measure the combined effects.

Standard requests CG18

The 29,014 applicants who had offers under both policies received $252.80 million less in total. That reduction outweighed the dollars added by new offers, even before counting lost offers. The average offer fell from $128,596 to $119,922.

One mortgage applicant received $655,000 under baseline and $615,000 under the challenger. Both policies made a counteroffer. That single borrower explains $40,000 of the reduction in the third row.

What else changed across the campaign?

Where did the applicants go when the review queue shrank?2,219 moved from manual review to decline. All 29,762 offers stayed in place.

CG06 changed the rule for handling a smaller offer that fails the lender’s tests. Baseline referred those cases to a person. The challenger declined them. Of 50,000 applicants, those 2,219 were the only ones whose decision changed. Reviews fell from 7,535 to 5,316, with identical dollars offered and identical modeled loss.

Follow the $43,880.83 personal loan through both policies to see where that rule took effect. An operational pilot could track whether human reviewers would have found a workable offer in any of the declined cases.

Compare the two review policiesInspect the personal loan

Why did a rate shock leave most default estimates unchanged?46,633 of 50,000 estimates held steady. The other 3,367 rose.

In the generator’s two-percentage-point rate shock, a payment could rise while staying in the same payment-to-income band. The model raises its risk multiplier when the payment crosses a band boundary.

Home equity application APP-00047266 crossed the 20% boundary. On this $250,000 interest-only line, the rate went from 12.8269% to 14.8269%. The rate increase added $250,000 × 2% ÷ 12 = $416.67 a month. The payment went from 18.84% to 21.78% of income, the multiplier rose from 1.25 to 1.45, and modeled default probability went from 22.85% to 26.51%.

Which borrowers account for the most loss dollars?Near-prime mortgages accounted for one-third of baseline modeled loss on offers.

Under baseline policy M2-01, 2,752 near-prime mortgage offers totaled $706.36 million. At 2.75 cents of modeled loss per dollar offered, that added up to $19.40 million of the portfolio’s $58.55 million.

Deep-subprime revolving credit lines had the highest loss per dollar among the 25 product and credit-score groups at 29.70 cents. That rate came from a single $6,000 counteroffer, contributing $1,782 of modeled loss. The rate measures risk per dollar. The offer count and total dollars show how much of the portfolio it affects.

How can expected loss rise when the lender offers less credit?Under stressed recoveries, offers fell by $245.12 million and modeled loss rose by $6.90 million.

Runs M2-31 and M2-32 held the baseline lending policy and each applicant’s modeled default probability fixed. The stress scenario assumed the lender recovers less when a secured borrower defaults. Across the same 50,000 applicants, offers fell from 29,762 to 29,197, while expected loss on those offers rose from $58.55 million to $65.45 million.

For mortgage application APP-00000053, the request was $278,694.49 and the model estimated a 34.3171% chance of default. Raising the share lost in default from 20% to 28% increased expected loss on the request from $19,127.97 to $26,779.16. The baseline run offered $145,000. Under stressed recoveries, the counteroffer failed and the request went to manual review.

Which questions would you take to a pilot?

Who takes the offer? Measure how often applicants accept a smaller amount, by product and risk band. The campaign counts offers. Acceptance and funding determine which offers become outstanding balances.

Does the expected loss match experience? Follow funded accounts over a common observation window. Allow time for defaults and recoveries to emerge, then compare realized losses with the model’s estimates.

Is the policy worth operating? Compare interest and fee income with funding costs, credit losses and servicing costs. Track the number of applications sent to review and the time each decision takes.

Code and evidence

Trace a number to its source

The campaign used two PostgreSQL programs in DBeaver. Module 1 generated 50,000 synthetic applications. Across 39 runs, Module 2 applied the lending policies and recorded each decision, its reasons and its modeled economics. Each of the 20 matched comparisons evaluated the same 50,000 application IDs on both sides and recorded zero mismatches in the source fields it checked.
