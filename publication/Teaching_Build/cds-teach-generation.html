Module 1 · Synthetic applications

Where did this borrower come from?

Start with application APP-00032476. To build its credit profile, the SQL combines the application ID, a population ID and 15 different labels to produce 15 repeatable numbers between zero and one. Each stands in for a draw from a distribution. Using the same IDs reproduces the draws, so a second policy can evaluate the same application.

  1. Assign a product and a credit score. One draw selects a product using the portfolio mix. Another selects a credit-score band. A third places the borrower within that band. This application becomes an auto loan with a near-prime score of 654.
  2. Build a financial profile. Each score band has an income range and a distribution. Applicants in the same band can earn different incomes, and neighboring bands can overlap. This applicant’s simulated annual income is $121,227.52. The engine also generates features such as debt burden, credit use and recent inquiries.
  3. Calculate affordability. For this $12,839.56 request, 48 monthly installments at a modeled 9.4304% annual rate give a $322.14 monthly payment. Divide by monthly income of $10,102.29 to get 3.19%. That is the share of gross monthly income this new payment would consume.
Reproduce the product and score assignments

Baseline assigns the first 30% of the product draw range to mortgages, the next 25% to revolving lines and the next 20% to auto loans. This application’s draw is 0.743735, inside the auto interval from 0.55 up to 0.75. Its separate score-band draw is 0.698823, inside the near-prime interval from 0.46 up to 0.70. For the 60 integer scores from 640 through 699, the score calculation is 640 + floor(60 × 0.241315) = 654. The draws shown here are rounded to six decimal places.

Why freeze the inputs? Keeping the population ID fixes the draws. Changing an assumption such as the product mix or interest rate can still change the generated application. To measure a policy change alone, both Module 2 policies read the same saved Module 1 scenario and population. The resulting comparison describes how the rules behave on these synthetic applicants. Its relevance to a particular bank depends on how closely the assumptions resemble that bank’s applicants.
